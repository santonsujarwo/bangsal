A building project is made up of connected stages. The exact process varies by project type, location, contract, and regulatory requirements, but the following outline is a useful starting point.

## Brief and feasibility

The client and project team clarify the intended outcome, site conditions, scope, budget expectations, and constraints. Early feasibility work helps identify questions that need to be resolved before detailed commitments are made.

## Design and coordination

Drawings, specifications, engineering input, and material choices are developed and coordinated. Decisions made at this stage can influence buildability, cost, sequencing, and maintenance.

## Pre-construction preparation

The team plans procurement, site access, temporary works where required, responsibilities, programme, and site procedures. Applicable approvals and permits should be confirmed with the relevant professionals and authorities.

## Construction and monitoring

Work is carried out in planned stages. Progress reviews, quality checks, safety processes, and clear communication help the team respond to issues and keep decisions documented.

## Completion and handover

The project team reviews completed work, records outstanding items, gathers relevant documentation, and explains operation or maintenance requirements as applicable.

A clear process does not remove every uncertainty, but it makes responsibilities and decisions easier to manage.

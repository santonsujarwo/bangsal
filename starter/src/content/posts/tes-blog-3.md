Starting a construction project is easier when the first decisions are organised. You do not need every drawing finalised before an initial conversation, but a clear brief helps everyone understand the outcome you want.

## 1. Define the purpose of the space

Write down how the building or renovated area should be used. Consider the people who will use it, the activities it needs to support, and what a successful result would look like.

## 2. Gather basic site information

Prepare the site address or area, available land or floor area, photographs, access details, and any known site restrictions. Existing drawings and survey information can also be useful.

## 3. Set an initial budget range

Think about the overall investment you are comfortable exploring. Include a contingency allowance and remember that scope, specification, location, and site conditions can affect costs.

## 4. Identify timing requirements

Note any preferred start date, important milestones, operational restrictions, or target completion window. A realistic programme needs to account for design decisions, procurement, approvals, and construction activities.

## 5. List the decisions still to be made

Separate confirmed requirements from open questions. This makes it easier to identify which specialist advice or project information is needed next.

## A useful first-meeting pack

- A short project description
- Site location and photographs
- Approximate dimensions or floor area
- Reference images or inspiration
- Initial budget range
- Preferred timeline and key constraints
